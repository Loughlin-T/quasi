Is Tolles–Lawson enough for an in-cabin magnetometer?
Compensation · DAF-MIT open flight data, flight 1002

Classic Tolles–Lawson removes 85–95 % of in-cabin interference, even from a calibration box flown 3 km above the survey. Aircraft telemetry halves what is left, but only if you low-pass it first.

How good does compensation need to be for magnetic navigation?
MagNav · particle filter over 115 km of real data

A compensated in-cabin sensor navigates as well as a tail stinger (20–35 m against 1 km of INS drift). The filter tolerates 200 nT of white noise, but uncompensated interference of similar size breaks it.

Dr. Loughlin Tuck, P.Eng.

Founder and principal consultant

Loughlin is a professional engineer and geophysicist who has spent more than a decade designing, flying and processing magnetic sensing systems. His doctoral research showed how to characterise and compensate the magnetic interference of uncrewed aircraft, and he has since led research programmes in magnetic anomaly detection, alternative navigation and quantum sensing.

He founded QuASI to give defence, survey and sensor companies direct access to that experience: someone who has worked at the sensor, the aircraft and the algorithm, and who can say plainly what will and won't work.
