How it works

Sensor noise is modelled as white noise of density S0 with a 1/f rise below the corner frequency fc, so its power spectral density is S0²(1 + fc/f). Integrating over the band [f1, f2] gives σ² = S0²[(f2 − f1) + fc ln(f2/f1)].

A dipole of moment m at range r produces a peak total-field anomaly of about k·(μ0/4π)·m/r³, where k runs from 1 (broadside) to 2 (along the dipole axis). Detection range is where that peak equals the required SNR times the total noise.

This is a first-order budget. It ignores the band-limiting of the anomaly itself (set the band to suit your speed and range: the anomaly's dominant frequency is roughly v/(2r)), heading error, and the processing gain of a matched-filter detector. For those, use the MAD simulator.
